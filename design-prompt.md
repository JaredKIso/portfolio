# Design prompt

Style chosen by a random roll over the 25 listed styles (`/dev/urandom` → index 9): **Material**.
The prompt below is the three-paragraph landing-page prompt that roll produced. The "concept"
is Jared Kell's personal portfolio rather than an invented business, since that is what the
page is for.

---

Design a single, cohesive one-page landing experience in the Material style: a personal
portfolio for an engineer who spends his days inside production incidents and his evenings
running his own infrastructure, now steering toward data engineering. Material at its best
feels like well-made paper under good light: calm, ordered, and quietly physical, where every
surface knows its height and every shadow tells you what can be touched. Visitors should
arrive feeling oriented at once, as if a competent colleague had laid the essentials on a
clean desk for them. As they scroll, the page should feel like a stack of cards being dealt in
a sensible order: who he is, what he has fixed, what he has built, how to reach him. Bring in
colour with intent: one confident primary tone carried through tonal surfaces, and small,
saturated accents on chips, metrics and focus states, so the page feels alive and optimistic
rather than clinical.

The philosophy is clarity through layered surfaces. Typography should feel trustworthy and
plain-spoken, a geometric sans that is generous in the headline and crisp in the detail, the
voice of someone who writes root-cause reports to a verifiable-facts-only standard. Motion
should feel physical and purposeful: cards that rise a few pixels when you approach them,
ripples that confirm a tap, sections that ease in with a short, decelerating glide, never a
flourish for its own sake. The emotional arc runs from quick confidence in the opening hero,
through earned respect as the case studies show real numbers and real diagnoses, to a sense
of a whole person in the self-hosted stack and side projects, and closes on an easy, warm
invitation to get in touch, one obvious action at the end of one continuous scroll.

Take inspiration from the feeling of a well-run operations room at a quiet hour: clear
status at a glance, nothing out of place, everything labelled. Think of the honesty of an
engineering notebook, the satisfaction of a precisely folded paper model, and mid-century
information design that made complex systems legible to anyone. These references should
lend the page a sense of tactile depth without heaviness, of order that never feels rigid,
and of craftsmanship that shows in spacing, alignment and restraint rather than decoration,
so the whole single page reads as the work of someone you would trust with your production
database.
